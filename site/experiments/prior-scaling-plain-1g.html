Count prior vs training on its books at 1 GB (plain 10.8M on 1.02G characters)

The control for a count prior counted from more text than the network can read in its budget: is mobius's prior counted from 1 GB of PG-19 books, fed to a 10.8M char transformer trained on the 20M split, worth more than training the same transformer on those books plus the 20M, for the same 50k steps?

baselineconcludedpositivecount-prioreval: legacy
Opened 2026-11-13Updated 2026-11-13
THE ANSWER SO FAR

Yes, at equal network steps, on one seed each. The plain 10.8M trained on the 1.02G characters reaches held-out 2.9058 PPL (1.5389 bits/char). The 10.8M with the 1 GB prior in its input and prior dropout 0.5 (mobius, rnd/unit-lattice) reaches 2.8559 (1.5139), 0.025 bits/char better, about 2.5 times the ~0.01 rerun noise. At 200M the same comparison was a tie (0.006). Going from 200M to 1 GB of books gained the prior model 0.038 and the plain model 0.019. The prior's lead shrinks over training (0.137 at 5k steps, 0.040 at 15k, 0.025 at 50k) but is not gone. The prior's counting (about 2.6 h of CPU) is not in the comparison.

prior-scaling-plain-1g

Status: concluded (2026-11-13). One run, in agpt at the user's request; the second half of prior-scaling-plain-220m, which found a tie at 200M of books.

Question

At 200M characters of books, the count prior with prior dropout tied a plain transformer trained on the same books (0.0057 bits/char, inside the noise). The user's view is that the prior's use is to feed a small model more text than it trains on. At 50k steps × 64 × 256 = 819M characters, the plain model can read 220M characters 3.7 times, so 200M of books was not more text than it could read. mobius has since counted a prior from 1 GB of books (pg19split20m_gated_d16_pg1g, 2,557 books), more than the model can read once in the same budget. This run is the matching control: the same plain transformer trained on those books.

Design

  • Code: prior_transformer_bytes.py, a copy of air's experiments/prior_scaling/prior_transformer.py (last change air 7c46036; imm's transformer_baseline at 43057d9) with two changes: imm's path is absolute, and the training text is held as a byte tensor (np.fromfile through a lookup table), with batches widened to int64. The original holds one int64 per character, 8 GB for this text. A 30-step CPU run printed the same output, character for character, from both scripts. --prior none; the other arguments are those of air's s10.8m_none and of the 220M run: 6 layers, 6 heads, width 384, block 256, dropout 0.2, batch 64, 50k iterations, lr 1e-3 to 1e-4, warmup 100, weight decay 0.1, seed 1337 (run.sh).
  • Config: air's pg19split20m_cfg.json with only run_id and train_input changed (config.json).
  • Training text: 1,019,779,493 characters, the first 999,779,493 of mobius's data/pg19/imm-split-20m/books.txt followed by imm's train_corpus.txt (20M). Those are its first 2,557 books, what the 1 GB prior counted (its extra_counts.chars). sha256 21eaf56afc24206a12648b8a72c38f39a4912ca2b2e4a1debd06c1c3f2e0fe72; all 96 characters are in the split's vocabulary.
  • How much it reads: 819M target characters, 0.80 per character of text. Batches start at uniform random offsets, so about e^−0.80 = 45% of the characters are never a target. On the 220M text that share was 2.4%.
  • Evaluation: air's protocol, unchanged (the protocol field matches air's runs): every held-out character after the first of the split's 250k held-out characters (50 pieces of PG-19 test books), with up to 256 characters of context, every 2,500 steps.
  • The prior run compared with: mobius ran @air's pd_s10.8m_prior_in (prior in the input and on the logits, prior dropout 0.5, seed 1337, 50k steps) on the 1 GB prior's rows, reported in mobius's rnd/unit-lattice/README.md (2026-11-12 and 2026-11-13 sections). Its curve is read from that run's result file in mobius's working files, not committed anywhere.

Reading

  • At 1 GB the prior beats training on its books. At equal network steps the prior model ends 0.025 bits/char ahead. That is about 2.5 times @air's same-seed rerun spread at this size (up to about 0.01). It is one seed each.
  • The prior made twice the use of the extra books. From 200M to 1 GB of books the prior model gained 0.038 and the plain model 0.019. The plain model's gain over the 220M model reached 0.017 by 15k steps and stayed near there to the end, while 45% of its text was never a target.
  • The plain model now beats the 200M prior, by 0.013 at 50k steps, after trailing it until about 30k. More books trained in beat fewer books counted.
  • The prior's lead still shrinks. It falls from 0.137 at 5k steps to 0.040 at 15k and 0.025 at 50k, more slowly after 15k. At 200M the same lead fell from 0.062 to 0.006. Whether a longer budget would close the gap at 1 GB is not measured. The plain model would also reach more of its text in a longer run.
  • The two document-row runs land on the same 1.514, 0.025 ahead of this model as well. mobius's document side is counted from the 20M alone, so it is a gain that does not come from more books at all.

Caveats

  • One seed per run. The 0.025 gap is above the measured rerun spread, not far above it.
  • Equal network steps, not equal compute. The prior's rows took mobius about an hour of matching and about 1.6 h more of projections and folds on CPU, at a peak of 4.4–6.5 GB (mobius's notes). The networks trained in 3,752 s (plain) and 3,934 s (prior), with evaluation. A plain run given the prior's extra time as more steps has not been run.
  • Books are joined end to end, so a 256-character block can span two books, as in air's and the 220M runs.
  • The 20M split is 2% of this training text against 9% of the 220M text. The held-out pieces come from PG-19's test books, not from the 20M split's books, so the split has no special claim on them.
  • Not lm-eval byte PPL: air's protocol, the same for every row. eval: legacy in agpt's terms.
  • s10.8m_none_books1g.pt (the final weights) is local and not committed.

Files

config.json, run.sh, prior_transformer_bytes.py, s10.8m_none_books1g.json (the result, with the curve), s10.8m_none_books1g.log. Run in slot 14 of @air's queue (tools/slots), 10:02–11:04 UTC 2026-11-13, at nice 19, idle I/O priority, 2 CPU threads; 472 MiB of GPU memory, about 2.2 GB resident.

Count prior vs training on its books (plain 10.8M on 220M characters)

The user's framing of the count prior as a data resource: is a prior counted from 200M characters of books, fed to a 10.8M char transformer trained on a 20M split, worth more than simply training the same transformer on those books plus the 20M, at the same compute?

baselineconcludedmixedcount-prioreval: legacy
Opened 2026-11-13Updated 2026-11-13
THE ANSWER SO FAR

At 50k steps it is a tie for the best prior form and a loss for the other. The plain 10.8M trained on the 220M characters reaches held-out 2.9442 PPL (1.5579 bits/char), 0.0455 bits/char better than on the 20M alone. @air's prior model with the prior in its input and prior dropout 0.5 reaches 2.9326, only 0.0057 bits/char better, inside the ~0.01 run-to-run noise at this size; with the prior on the logits only it reaches 2.9890, 0.022 worse. The prior's lead is early: 0.062 bits/char at 5k steps, shrinking steadily to 0.006 at 50k, while the books-trained model's gain over 20M keeps growing. One seed each.

prior-scaling-plain-220m

Status: concluded (2026-11-13). One run, in agpt for the user; the test @air proposed (air's .ai/memory/todo.md, 2026-11-12) for the user's framing that the count prior is a data resource whose value is how much information it feeds, and how fast, compared with a model learning the same text.

Question

@air's prior-scaling study (air experiments/prior_scaling/, one seed per run) put mobius's count prior, built from 200M characters of PG-19 books plus the 20M training split, into imm's char transformer trained on the 20M split alone. At 10.8M parameters the prior helped only with prior dropout 0.5: 2.9326 held-out PPL (prior in the input and on the logits) against 3.0386 for the plain transformer. But that plain transformer never saw the books. The fair comparison for "the prior is a data resource" is the plain transformer trained on the books themselves, at the same compute.

Design

  • Code: air's experiments/prior_scaling/prior_transformer.py, unchanged (last change air 7c46036; imm's transformer_baseline at 43057d9), with --prior none. That reproduces imm's transformer_baseline.py. Arguments are those of air's s10.8m_none: 6 layers, 6 heads, width 384, block 256, dropout 0.2, batch 64, 50k iterations, lr 1e-3 to 1e-4, warmup 100, weight decay 0.1, seed 1337 (run.sh). The output's model field names a gated prior, but that is a fixed string in the script; prior=none is in the log.
  • Config: air's pg19split20m_cfg.json with only run_id and train_input changed (config.json).
  • Training text: 219,949,702 characters, the first 199,949,702 of mobius's data/pg19/imm-split-20m/books.txt followed by imm's train_corpus.txt (20M). Those are its first 500 books, all from PG-19's train part, exactly what the books prior counted (pg19split20m_gated_d16_pg200m.json: extra_counts.chars 199,949,702, books 500). sha256 f5815b65bdb8354126b87ac21dd16aa2faeb832be383c050fcebda9332897280. All 96 characters are in the split's vocabulary.
  • Same compute: 50k steps × 64 × 256 = 819M characters, about 3.7 passes over the 220M (the 20M runs make about 41). Training took 3,748 s, against 3,823 s (plain, 20M), 3,967 s (prior in the input) and 3,877 s (prior on the logits), all with evaluation. The prior's own cost, counting the 200M and building its rows, is not included in those times.
  • Evaluation: air's protocol: every held-out character after the first of the split's 250k held-out characters (50 pieces of PG-19 test books), with up to 256 characters of context, every 2,500 steps.

Reading

  • The best prior form ties with training on the books. The prior in the input with dropout ends 0.0057 bits/char ahead of the books-trained model. @air measured same-seed reruns at 10.8M differing by up to about 0.01 bits/char, so this is a tie on one seed each.
  • The prior feeds information faster, not more. It is 0.06 bits/char ahead at 5k steps, and the lead shrinks at every later evaluation. The books-trained model's gain over the 20M model grows instead, from 0.008 at 10k to 0.0455 at 50k. With the prior on the logits only, the books-trained model passes it at about 35k steps.
  • Where it goes next is a guess. The books-trained model had seen its text 3.7 times, the prior models theirs 41 times, and every curve is still falling at the end of its learning-rate decay. A longer budget would likely favour the model with more text left. That is a reading of the trends, not a measurement.
  • My earlier guess was off. On 2026-11-12 I expected the plain model on 220M to gain more than 0.05 bits/char over the 20M model and, by implication, to beat the prior model. It gained 0.0455, and did not beat the best prior model.

Caveats

  • One seed per run. The decisive difference (0.0057) is below the rerun noise.
  • Books are joined end to end, so a 256-character block can span two books; air's 20M runs join their 800 pieces the same way.
  • Not lm-eval byte PPL: air's protocol, the same for every row of the table. eval: legacy in agpt's terms.
  • The prior's preprocessing is not counted in the compute comparison.
  • s10.8m_none_books220m.pt (the final weights) is local and not committed.

Files

config.json, run.sh, s10.8m_none_books220m.json (the result, with the curve), s10.8m_none_books220m.log. Run in a slot from @air's queue (tools/slots), at nice 19, idle I/O priority, 2 CPU threads.

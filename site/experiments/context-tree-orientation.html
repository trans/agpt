Context-tree orientation

Does the orientation of AGPT's context tree matter for training, the prefix trie (oldest character at the root, as AGPT has always built it) or the suffix tree (newest at the root, each subtree one target's prediction problem), and if so, why?

experimentconcludedpositiverecurrenteval: legacy
Opened 2026-10-03Updated 2026-11-10
THE ANSWER SO FAR

Yes, and the prefix trie is the right one, for a reason nobody had seen (a minor breakthrough, with a proviso to double-check). A pd=1 step uses one root-child subtree. Prefix-trie units group contexts by their oldest, least predictive character, so their targets are well mixed and they train as well as shuffled minibatches with the same number of steps. Suffix-tree units group by the newest, most predictive character, so each step is a target-sorted batch. With the tree removed and one GRU, that grouping alone costs about two thirds of the suffix tree's pd=1 gap; the newest-first reading costs the rest. GRU d64 depth 8, diagnostic held-out PPL, mostly one seed.

Context-tree orientation

Design and motivation: todo/context-tree-orientation.md. In short, the prefix trie groups contexts by their oldest characters. The suffix tree groups them by their newest. Both have the same nodes, counts and loss, but in the suffix tree a node's whole subtree predicts the same target as the node, and the parent is the backoff context. The derivation predicted that the trie's gradient sums (G_p = g_p + Σ J^T G_child) would be more coherent in the suffix tree.

Conclusion (2026-11-10): a minor breakthrough

The user called the strand on 2026-11-10, labelled it a minor breakthrough, and attached a proviso to double-check and polish it (the Proviso section, at the end).

AGPT has used the prefix trie from the start, and its partitioned training (pd≥1, one optimizer step per root-child subtree) has worked. It worked for a reason nobody had identified. A prefix-trie pd=1 unit is every context that starts with one character. The oldest character of a context says the least about the next one, so the units come out well mixed in their targets, and stepping through them behaves like stepping through shuffled minibatches. The transposed tree, the suffix tree, groups contexts by their newest character, which says the most. Its units are target-sorted batches, and partitioned training on them is clearly worse. The user had long expected AGPT's training to show exactly this sorting problem, and never understood why it didn't show more clearly. It didn't because AGPT, without anyone choosing it for this reason, never grouped that way.

The evidence (GRU d64, depth 8, Adam at 3e-3, 65 steps per epoch, diagnostic held-out NLL):

  • Target skew of the units (mean KL of a unit's targets from the corpus's): suffix-tree 0.861 nats, prefix-trie 0.090, shuffled minibatches 0.0002.

  • The grouping causes most of the gap. In a no-trie control, the same GRU takes the same steps and only the grouping changes. At 500 epochs:

    • oldest-first GRU: suffix-tree grouping 1.759, prefix-trie grouping 1.628;
    • newest-first GRU: 1.838 and 1.681.

    The grouping accounts for about two thirds of the October pd=1 gap, and the newest-first reading for the rest.

  • Prefix-trie units are as good as shuffled minibatches. With equal numbers of equal-sized steps they agree on train loss to 0.001 at epoch 100, under a constant and a cosine schedule. Held-out differences are about 0.01 and change sign with the epoch: 1.628 and 1.632 (two seeds) against 1.632 for start-labelled random batches at 500 epochs; 1.704 against 1.714 for shuffled windows under cosine at 100.

The user suspected the prefix units' remaining correlation (skew 0.09 against ~0) explained part of AGPT's gap to vanilla minibatch training (rnd/vanilla-attn-torch). At an equal number of equal-sized steps, this control finds no such cost, so that gap more likely comes from elsewhere. The plainest candidate is step count: vanilla training takes many more, smaller steps per epoch. Their reading (2026-11-10) is that getting further needs something past the tree, though they suspect something is still being missed.

Setup

src/tools/agpt_context_tree_coherence.py. Carved Shakespeare split data/.splits/4fa9aec1db6b3aea (train slice). The node table is every context of length 1–8 with its next-character counts: 1,495,879 nodes and 8,477,036 events, shared by both trees. There are 1,401 depth-2 units, as in rnd/gradient-population Experiment 1.

model tree node state reads readout
fwd prefix trie, parent = s[:-1] newest char (left-to-right GRU) W h_s + b
rev suffix tree, parent = s[1:] oldest char (newest-first GRU) W h_s + b
rev-res suffix tree oldest char z_parent + W h_s + b

All three use d_model 64, GRUCell and the same initial weights (seed 1). Each takes 2,000 full-batch Adam steps at lr 3e-3 on the exact gradient of the same objective, with no partitioning, so the update schedule is identical. The gradient is computed one depth at a time, leaves first, with the children's summed adjoint injected. It matches plain autograd to 1e-16 in float64 for all three models, and the depth-2 unit gradients plus the depth-1 own losses sum to the full gradient to 1e-15.

Held-out is a diagnostic: mean NLL of each next character with up to 8 characters of context, within each of the 10 held-out chunks. It is not the canonical lm-eval protocol.

Coherence, as defined in the tool docstring:

  • node: at node p, the parts are the readout adjoint at h_p and each child's J^T G_child. The kept fraction is Σ_p ‖G_p‖ / Σ_p Σ‖parts‖, per depth.
  • unit: parameter-space gradients of the depth-2 subtrees, as in Experiment 1. ρ global is what a pd=0 step keeps of them, ρ per-root is what a pd=1 step keeps (event-weighted average over roots), and pd1 is ρ over the 65 whole root subtrees.

Run: 20261003T051146-gru-d64-depth8-fullbatch-adam3e-3-2000/ (run.log, summary.json). The tool was uncommitted at launch on top of cac6c86. About 8 minutes of training per model.

Reading

  1. The prediction held at initialization only. At step 0 the suffix-tree sums keep more of their parts at every depth, and more the deeper the node (0.80 / 0.86 / 0.91 against a flat ~0.72). After training, a gain remains at depth 1, depth 2 is lower, and depth 3 ties.
  2. Under full-batch training the tree is bookkeeping. The exact gradient of a given f_θ is the same whatever tree computes it (SGD-equivalence). So this run compares reading directions of the GRU, which is the todo's experiment 1 more than its experiment 2. The newest-first GRU loses at depth 3 and beyond. That is consistent with recency inversion: it must carry the newest character through every older step. The residual readout recovers about 40% of the held-out gap.
  3. Coherence of an exact sum is not lost gradient. At a full-batch stationary point the summed gradient is zero by definition, so falling global coherence partly just means convergence. This also qualifies Experiment 1's reading ("cancellation grows with training"). Coherence matters for partitioned updates, where units step in sequence, not for the exact full-batch step.

pd=1: one Adam step per root-child subtree

Run 20261003T062907-gru-d64-depth8-pd1-adam3e-3-500ep/. Each step uses one of the 65 root subtrees, with its event-mean gradient (as bin/agpt_train_v2 fires it). Unit order is reshuffled each epoch, with the same sequence for all three models. The subtree gradients sum to the full gradient to 1e-16 (float64 check). The units differ by orientation: "contexts starting with c" in the prefix trie, "contexts ending in c" in the suffix tree. The root masses are the same.

  1. Partitioning helps the prefix trie about twice as much. At epoch 300, pd=1 improves held-out NLL by 0.20 for fwd and 0.10 for rev. With the residual readout it hurts after ~100 epochs: 2.214 against 1.820 at pd=0. Its loss rises with depth beyond 4, because the summed corrections compound.
  2. Why: each suffix-tree unit is one prediction problem. Every node under root c predicts the character after c, so a pd=1 step is a target-sorted minibatch: its targets are skewed toward what follows c (mean KL to the corpus target distribution 0.86 nats, against 0.09 for prefix-trie units; see the grouping control below). It pulls the shared weights toward one unit's targets and the next step undoes it. (The 2026-10-03 version of this note said "one target class", which overstates it: the targets vary, but they are heavily skewed.) The depth-2 unit gradients show the structure. For rev, cosine within a root is 8–30× the cosine across roots from epoch 10 on (for example 0.103 vs 0.013 at epoch 300). For fwd it is about 1.8× (0.015 vs 0.008). Grouping by the least predictive character is what makes prefix-trie units well mixed.
  3. This is a property of the tree, not the GRU. Any f_θ that shares computation in the suffix tree gets these units. So an attention f_θ would face the same pd=1 penalty. With the GRU, the grouping control below puts the units' share of the penalty at about two thirds. Only an attention model's pd=0 behaviour (reading direction without recency inversion) is open.

pd=1 epochs are about 0.55 s in this Python tool against 0.23 s at pd=0 (per-unit overhead). The node work per epoch is the same.

Grouping control: the units, not the reading direction (2026-11-10)

The pd=1 comparison above changes two things at once, the units and the GRU's reading direction. src/tools/agpt_unit_grouping_control.py takes the tree away. Every context in the same node table (1,495,879 nodes, same counts) is an example whose GRU state is recomputed from its own characters, so any grouping can be paired with either reading direction. Steps are as at pd=1: 65 groups per epoch in a fresh random order (the orientation tool's RNG sequence), an event-mean gradient, Adam at 3e-3, 500 epochs, seed 1.

grouping one step holds which is target skew
first the contexts with the same oldest char a prefix-trie pd=1 unit 0.0899
last the contexts with the same newest char a suffix-tree pd=1 unit 0.8606
window corpus windows, assigned at random each epoch a shuffled minibatch 0.0002
start windows grouped by a random label on their start position prefix-unit clumping without the char sort 0.0002

Target skew is the event-weighted mean over groups of KL(group's target distribution ‖ corpus target distribution), in nats. The random groupings draw group sizes from the prefix units' event shares, so step sizes match in distribution. A window is one event: context train[i:i+L], target train[i+L], 1 ≤ L ≤ 8.

Checks. The fwd/first and rev/last cells are the October pd=1 runs recomputed without the tree. They match to four decimals through epoch 10 (repro-fwd-first.log, repro-rev-last.log; stopped there to save GPU time). The event (window) code path, with each window labelled by its oldest char, reproduces fwd/first exactly at epoch 3 (2.3147). A first random control assigned whole nodes to groups. It was lumpy, because the 65 depth-1 nodes carry an eighth of all events, so a small group could be a single heavy context (skew 0.034). It was stopped at epoch 30 (aborted-fwd-random-node/), and the window-level control replaced it.

  1. The grouping causes most of the suffix tree's pd=1 penalty. At 500 epochs suffix grouping costs 0.131 nats with the oldest-first GRU (1.759 against 1.628) and 0.157 with the newest-first GRU (1.838 against 1.681). Reading newest-first costs 0.053 with prefix grouping and 0.080 with suffix grouping. Of the October gap (0.211), the units account for about 0.14 and the reading direction for about 0.07, and the two compound slightly. With either GRU, suffix grouping raises the train loss at every depth from 3 on, by 0.09–0.16.

  2. Prefix-trie units tie shuffled minibatches. With equal numbers of equal-sized steps, train loss agrees to 0.001 at epoch 100 under both schedules: 1.8446 against 1.8455 constant, 1.8547 against 1.8539 cosine. The held-out differences are about 0.01 and change sign:

    • at epoch 100, prefix units lead (1.677 and 1.682 against 1.694 and 1.692; cosine 1.704 against 1.714);
    • at epochs 300 and 500, start-labelled random batches tie or lead (1.631 against 1.640 and 1.638; 1.632 against 1.628 and 1.632).

    The prefix units' leftover skew (0.09 against 0.0002) costs nothing measurable at this scale. Shuffled batches are ahead only in the first ~10 epochs (2.183 against 2.315 at epoch 3).

  3. Clumping by start position costs nothing. Keeping together all the windows that start at one position (start) tracks fully shuffled windows (window) to within about 0.01 through epoch 100. So neither part of a prefix unit's structure costs anything measurable: neither the clumping by start position nor the sort by oldest character.

  4. Shuffled windows at a constant 3e-3 hit an instability. The window cell's train loss rose after epoch 100 (1.846, then 1.893 at 300) and only partly recovered (1.835 at 500, held-out 1.666). Start-labelled random batches at the same LR did not do this (train 1.799 at 300, 1.804 at 500). Both have near-zero skew, and each has one seed, so this is unexplained. The cosine runs avoid it, and the 500-epoch window number should not be read as a property of shuffled batches.

Proviso: double-check before building on this

The user called this strand on 2026-11-10 as a minor breakthrough, with a proviso to double-check and polish it. These are the checks, roughly in order of how much each could change the reading:

  1. Seeds. Only fwd/first has two seeds (5.093 and 5.114). The random cells, the 2×2 cells and the cosine runs have one each.
  2. An attention f_θ. The grouping belongs to the tree, so any f_θ trained on suffix-tree units gets target-sorted steps. How much that costs an attention model is unmeasured. A cheaper test than a suffix-tree trainer is this control with an attention f_θ over the window: the grouping needs no tree. The v2 trainer would also need suffix-tree targets (todo, "Trainer targets").
  3. The CUDA trainers' unit order. v2 visits units in the same order every epoch unless experimental.unit_order_seed is set (src/cudax/yaml_config_v2.cuh:22, default −1). These Python runs reshuffle every epoch. v1's --shuffle-order gained about 2% at pd>1 (rnd/cap-folding), which fits this result: a fixed order is a mild sort. Measure it on v2 at pd=1.
  4. Depth 16, pd=2/3, and a longer warmup-cosine LR. Everything here is depth 8, and mostly at a constant 3e-3. The cosine check runs only 100 epochs. At pd=2, prefix units share their two oldest characters, which still say little about the target. Suffix units would share the two newest, which say a lot.
  5. Canonical eval. Every number here is the diagnostic held-out NLL. Anything cited elsewhere needs bin/agpt_experiment.
  6. Interference between unit steps (todo, step 1) was not measured. With this control it is less needed.

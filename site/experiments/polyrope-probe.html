PolyRoPE probe (window form)

The user's PolyRoPE design in its window form: if every symbol carries a damped harmonic signature of where it occurred in the last 1,024 characters, can a small model read it for prediction, and how much of exact attention's gain over the same reach does it recover?

THE ANSWER SO FAR

Not tested, because the controls left nothing to recover. At d64/L2, exact attention gains only 0.0025 nats/char from layer-2 offsets 128-1,023 on PG-19 books (0.006-0.009 on Tiny Shakespeare), against 0.0619 (0.03) from offsets 16-127. That is about one seed's noise, so no channel reading those offsets could show reach at this size, and the channel arms (built and checked) were not run. This tests only an in-context reading of PolyRoPE. The design's original notion, signatures of a token's positions across the whole corpus, is not tested here.

polyrope-probe

Status: concluded (2026-11-13). Controls only: exact attention at reaches 128 and 1,024 and the 16-window, on Tiny Shakespeare and on PG-19. The channel arms were not run, because the controls showed there was nothing beyond offset 128 for them to recover at this size.

What this probe is, and what it isn't

The user's design document ("PolyRoPE: Learning Semantics from Corpus Structure", 2026-11-12, passed on by @mobius; not in this repo) proposes giving each vocabulary token a spectral signature of all its positions in the corpus,

H_v(ω) = Σ_{p : C[p] = v} e^{iωp},

so that pair structure comes out of products: conj(H_a(ω)) H_b(ω) holds every displacement between an occurrence of a and one of b, and its inverse transform is their cross-correlation R_ab(d). Its principle: don't make the network rediscover structure that can be extracted directly. The document's programme runs from checking the mathematics (Phases 1–3) through signatures as enriched embeddings in ordinary training (Phase 5) to training on the vocabulary of signatures alone (Phases 6–7).

Form Where the structure comes from Tested?
Corpus form (§3.2, Phases 1–3) H_v over every occurrence in the corpus; pairs from cross-spectra @mobius, 2026-11-12: character bigrams recovered from K random frequencies of 4.2M PG-19 characters have a median error of 20% at K = 1,024 and 10% at K = 16,384, which is 330× the bigram table's storage. That fails Phase 1's criterion (useful pairs in less storage).
Window form H_v over the last W characters, at each position This probe. Built and checked; controls run; channel arms not run.
Enriched embedding (Phase 5) E_poly(v) = Combine(E(v), H_v) in ordinary training Not tested.
Structural queries, vocabulary-only training (Phases 6–7) training on (v, H_v) entries Not tested.

After mobius's corpus-form result, the user chose a window instead (2026-11-12), and this probe tests that. The window form is a far channel with one-hot content. So what has actually run is the channel approach: rnd/far-channel-probe (learned content, reach 128, 65% of exact attention's gain recovered), plus this probe's controls. Those controls cap what any in-context reader can gain at this size, whatever its readout. They say nothing about the corpus-level notion, which supplies statistics from text the model never sees in its context. That makes it the same kind of resource as the count prior.

Why the corpus form fails, and what would test the notion. Give attention the signatures as features with RoPE's rotation by the displacement Δ. A query on a at t and a key on b at t − Δ then score

Re Σ_ω c_ω conj(H_a(ω)) H_b(ω) e^{iωΔ} = Σ_d R_ab(d) κ(d + Δ),   κ(x) = Σ_ω c_ω cos(ωx).

That is the corpus count of b at Δ before a, blurred by a kernel κ. With K frequencies, κ's sidelobes also collect the pairs at every other displacement up to the corpus length. That is mobius's error, and the decoding limit in notes/seq-len-extension/superposition-softmax-limit.md §4. For characters the spectral compression isn't needed: the exact R_ab(d) for d < 1,024 is 96² × 1,024 ≈ 9.4M counts on PG-19. So the notion itself, corpus displacement structure supplied to attention, can be tested exactly with counted tables. mobius has proposed distance co-occurrence counted from the books (mail to @agpt, 2026-11-12). The compression matters only at word-level vocabularies. By the results below, such a bias would matter mostly at offsets below 128. That is where the context carries information, and there it would act as a data resource, as the count prior does.

Design

src/tools/agpt_polyrope_probe.py (af81726; fd592ad adds the PG-19 options). It is the far-channel probe's harness: no trie, and a plain minibatch-Adam transformer with the AGPT d64/L2 layout (4 heads, d_ff 256, pre-LN, RoPE with interleaved pairs, base 10000, ReLU, untied head). Layer 1 attends over offsets 0–15. The arms differ only in layer 2:

Arm Layer 2 Params (Shakespeare) Run?
w16 softmax over offsets 0–15 (baseline) 108,481 yes
exact softmax over offsets 0 .. R−1 (upper bound); R = 128 or 1,024 108,481 yes
hidden 16-window, plus a far channel over 16 .. R−1 with keys from the layer input (far-channel probe's cq) 120,965 no
char the same far channel, keys and values from a learned 16-dim embedding of the character only 120,037 no
unit the same, one entry per complete unit at its last character 184,533 no

The far channel (the arms not run), per head, for the query at t and an entry at p, Δ = t − p, 16 ≤ Δ < R:

a[t, p] = φ(qf_t) · φ(k_p) · w_t(Δ),     w_t(Δ) = |Σ_j b_{t,j} λ_j^(Δ−16)|²,
λ_j = e^{−1/P_j} e^{2πi/P_j},  P = 8, 16, ..., 1,024   (8 constant-Q atoms)
far_t = Σ_p a[t, p] v_p / Σ_p a[t, p]

For char and unit the content depends only on the symbol s_p, so the channel's exact recurrent state can be kept per symbol type: H_v(μ) = Σ over entries p of v of μ^(t−p−16), for the 64 products μ = λ_j conj(λ_l). That is the user's windowed signature, damped. The state is 64 reals per symbol type, fixed whatever the reach: 65 × 64 ≈ 4.2k for Shakespeare's characters.

Units (the unit arm) are cut as mobius's UnitCuts cuts them (ported in unit_ends): at the boundaries between letters, punctuation and whitespace, with an apostrophe after a letter counted as a letter and a single space joining the unit after it. The 4,095 commonest training units get their own ids. On Shakespeare's training text there are 15,800 distinct units, 270,701 in all, 3.91 characters on average, and the ids cover 93.6% of them.

Protocol

  • Training: Adam, lr 1.5e-3, 500 warmup steps, then a cosine decay to 10%. Each step is 4 blocks of 2,048 characters (8,192 targets) with every position scored, for 30k steps (246M targets). On PG-19, imm's 20M split (800 pieces of 25,000 characters from as many train books), blocks stay inside one piece (--piece-chars 25000). That is 232 passes over Shakespeare's 1.06M training characters and 12 over PG-19's 20M.
  • Evaluation (diagnostic, not lm-eval): each held-out target is scored once, with at least 1,040 preceding characters where its chunk has them, whatever the arm's reach, so every run is scored on the same targets. Shakespeare: the canonical split's 10 chunks of 5,576 characters (55,750 targets). PG-19: 50 pieces of 5,000 characters from as many test books (249,950). NLL is also reported by the target's position in its chunk: 1–30, 31–1,038, and 1,039 to the end (the main comparison: 45,370 targets on Shakespeare, 198,050 on PG-19).
  • Order of runs: controls first. The channel arms were to run only if exact attention at reach 1,024 clearly beat reach 128 (the far-channel probe's rule: a control that can't show a gain makes the test meaningless).

Checks (at start-up of every run):

  • Changing inputs more than 1,038 characters back changes no arm's output (max change 0.0). Changing offsets 31–1,038 leaves w16 unchanged (0.0) and changes exact at reach 1,024.
  • The per-symbol recurrence H_v(μ) matches the far channel's parallel form to a relative 4e-7 (float32). Beyond the reach, the far arms' outputs change by at most 1.5e-7 in float32 and by exactly 0 in float64 (2026-11-12 CPU smoke tests).
  • The byte encoder used since fd592ad gives the same ids as the per-character dictionary on both corpora.

Cutting the reach-1,024 model's attention at evaluation

The same weights, with layer 2's span cut (CPU, --eval-only, rescore/exact-r1024-s42-at* and rescore/pg19-exact-r1024-s42-at*), on targets 1,039 to the end:

Layer-2 span at evaluation Shakespeare PG-19
1,024 (as trained) 1.3137 1.4249
512 1.3158 (+0.0021) 1.4259 (+0.0010)
256 1.3213 (+0.0076) 1.4287 (+0.0038)

Each octave of distance is worth less than the one before: on Shakespeare 0.0085 (offsets 128–255), 0.0055 (256–511) and 0.0021 (512–1,023); on PG-19 0.0049, 0.0028 and 0.0010. At 128 the cut costs more than the trained gap (0.0161 against 0.006–0.009 on Shakespeare, 0.0087 against 0.0025 on PG-19), because the cut model was trained to rely on keys it then loses.

Reading

  • At this size, almost nothing past offset 128 is used. Exact softmax over the same offsets, the probe's upper bound, gains 0.0025–0.009 nats/char from offsets 128–1,023. The trained-at-1,024 model uses every octave, but with value falling off with distance.
  • Books make near context worth more and far context worth less. On PG-19, offsets 16–127 are worth twice what they are on Shakespeare, and 128–1,023 less than half. This fits Khandelwal et al. (2018, "Sharp Nearby, Fuzzy Far Away"): LSTM language models are sensitive to word order only in the last ~20 tokens and use the distant past only as a rough sense of topic.
  • So the channel arms could not have shown reach. Their best case beyond offset 128 is a fraction of 0.0025–0.009, at or below the run-to-run spread of a single seed (w16's 4 seeds span 0.017 on Shakespeare). The user chose (2026-11-13) to measure the headroom on PG-19 before spending GPU time on them, and it came out smaller still.
  • This bounds the window form, not PolyRoPE. See "What this probe is, and what it isn't" above.

Caveats

  • Diagnostic evaluation, not lm-eval byte PPL. It is the same for every run, but its absolute values aren't comparable with canonical PPL.
  • One size, one seed. d64/L2 (about 110k parameters) at 30k steps. The PG-19 curves were still falling at 30k (12 passes), so a longer or larger run may use far context more. Whether the headroom grows with model size is the open question for the window form.
  • The Shakespeare reach-128 comparison crosses two trainers (the far-channel probe's 512-character blocks and this probe's 2,048). The PG-19 comparison doesn't: all three runs share this probe's trainer, seed and batches.
  • The short-context bucket (targets 1–30 of each chunk) reads worse for the long-reach models on PG-19 (1.6868 and 1.6763 against 1.6399 for w16), on 1,500 targets. It doesn't bear on the comparison.

Files

  • run_arms.sh: Shakespeare arms at reach 1,024 (used for the controls).
  • run_pg19_controls.sh: the PG-19 controls and the CPU cut rescoring.
  • rescore_far_channel.sh: rescoring the far-channel probe's w16 and exact checkpoints on this probe's targets.
  • Run directories hold args.json (including the start-up checks and git SHA) and metrics.json (the curve and final numbers). model.pt and train.log are not committed.

All heavy runs went through @air's slot queue (tools/slots in AIR), one heavy job at a time, at nice 19 with idle I/O priority and 2 CPU threads.

Next, if wanted

  • The original notion, exactly: corpus displacement counts R_ab(d) from the books, given to attention as a bias by displacement. This is mobius's proposed distance co-occurrence; see above.
  • The window form at a larger size: the same exact-attention controls at reach 128 and 1,024 on a model large enough that offsets 128–1,023 are worth more than a seed's noise. Only then can the channel arms show reach.

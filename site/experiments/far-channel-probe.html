Far-channel probe

Can the text older than a 16-character softmax window be read through a channel that is exact and recurrent but blurry in position (linear content, a smooth query-steered positional kernel), and how much of the gain of exact attention over the same offsets does it recover?

experimentconcludedpositiveattentioneval: legacy
Opened 2026-11-11Updated 2026-11-11
THE ANSWER SO FAR

Yes, partly. In layer 2 of a d64/L2 model, beside an exact 16-character window, a channel with linear content and a constant-Q positional kernel recovers 65% of the gain of exact softmax over the same offsets (16-127). It falls short of exact by 0.0104 +/- 0.0009 nats on every one of 4 seeds. A recency-only kernel recovers 48%. The channel is exact and recurrent with a fixed state, but at this reach that state is no smaller than the keys and values it replaces.

far-channel-probe

Status: concluded (2026-11-11). Four arms × 4 seeds at 30k steps.

Question

The softmax-limit note (notes/seq-len-extension/superposition-softmax-limit.md) shows that a superposed state can't cheaply reproduce a sharp softmax over occurrences. A channel that is exact and recurrent has to give something up. The user's point (2026-11-10): a blurry position is all that's really needed, as long as it stays ordinal. The note's §5 kernel is blurry in position. In layer 1, though, its values are single characters, so it would return a positionally weighted bag of characters. In layer 2 the keys depend on context, and exactness there needs linear content. The user agreed to linear content (2026-11-10).

So: put such a channel in layer 2, beside an exact 16-character window, and compare it with exact softmax over the same offsets.

Design

src/tools/agpt_far_channel_probe.py. No trie. A plain minibatch-Adam transformer with the AGPT d64/L2 layout (4 heads, d_ff 256, pre-LN, RoPE with interleaved pairs, base 10000, ReLU, untied head). Layer 1 always attends over offsets 0–15. The arms differ only in layer 2:

Arm Layer 2 Receptive field Params
w16 softmax over offsets 0–15 (baseline) 30 108,481
exact softmax over offsets 0–127 (upper bound) 142 108,481
decay softmax over 0–15, plus far channel over 16–127, recency kernel 142 118,885
cq the same, constant-Q kernel 142 120,965

Spans compound across layers: layer 2 at t reads layer-1 states up to its span back, and each of those reads 15 more. So w16 already reaches 30 characters, and the other arms reach 142.

The far channel, per head, for the query at t and a key at p, Δ = t − p:

a[t, p] = φ(qf_t) · φ(kf_p) · w_t(Δ),        16 ≤ Δ < 128,  φ(z) = elu(z) + 1
far_t   = Σ_p a[t, p] v_p / Σ_p a[t, p]
out_t   = (softmax over offsets 0–15) + gate_h · far_t,     gate_h starts at 1

qf and kf are the channel's own projections, and v is the layer's value. The content term has no position: position enters only through the kernel, which the query steers.

  • decay: w_t(Δ) = Σ_a π_{t,a} e^{−(Δ−16)/τ_a}, with π_t a softmax over 8 atoms and τ = 2, 4, …, 256. It is completely monotone, so it expresses recency only.
  • cq: w_t(Δ) = |Σ_j b_{t,j} λ_j^{Δ−16}|², with b_t complex and 8 constant-Q atoms λ_j = e^{−1/P_j} e^{2πi/P_j}, P = 4, 8, …, 512. It is nonnegative, and it can put a bump at a chosen distance, with a width that grows with the distance.

Both kernels are finite sums of exponentials in Δ, so the channel is exactly recurrent: one state Σ_p μ^{t−p} φ(kf_p) v_pᵀ (and its normalizer) per atom μ, 8 atoms for decay and 64 products λ_j conj(λ_l) for cq. Training uses the parallel form, which is the same sum.

Protocol

  • Training: Adam, lr 1.5e-3, 500 warmup steps, then a cosine decay to 10%. Each step is 16 blocks of 512 characters from the canonical split's train_corpus.txt, with every position scored (8,192 targets). 30k steps is about 246M targets, about 1.5× the ~170M at which vanilla seq-16 attention plateaued (rnd/vanilla-attn-torch).
  • Evaluation (diagnostic, not lm-eval): each of the 55,750 targets in the 10 held-out chunks is scored once, with at least 144 preceding characters where the chunk has them. NLL is also reported by the target's position in its chunk: 1–30 (short context for every arm), 31–142 (full context for w16 only), and 143 to the end (full context for every arm: the main comparison).
  • Order of runs: the controls (w16, exact) first. The far arms are run only if exact clearly beats w16 at this budget, because a control that can't show the known gain from context makes the test meaningless (rnd/superposed-tail-rope).

Checks (run at start-up in every run, 2026-11-11 smoke tests):

  • Changing inputs more than 142 characters back changes no arm's output (max change 0.0).
  • Changing inputs at offsets 31–142 leaves w16 unchanged (0.0) and changes every other arm.
  • The far channel's recurrence over atom states matches the parallel form to a relative 4e-7 (decay, cq; float32).
  • Each held-out target is scored exactly once (55,750).

Pooled over seeds, cq recovers 65% of exact's gain over w16 (per seed 54%, 69%, 71%, 61%), and decay 48% (16%, 69%, 30%, 61%). All heads use the channel: final gates 0.25–0.40 (cq) and 0.27–0.44 (decay) on every seed.

The control holds. exact beats w16 on every seed, and its curve is below w16's at every evaluation from 4k steps on. All curves are flat from about 20k steps, with held-out well above train loss, so the runs are converged.

Reading.

  • The far past can be read through a blurry, exact, recurrent channel. Both kernels beat the window on every seed. Older text helps, and a channel with linear content and only an ordinal, smooth sense of position delivers most of that help.
  • It is not exact attention. cq falls short of exact by a steady 0.010 nats (standard error 0.0009 over 4 seeds). That is the cost of linear content and a smooth kernel at this size.
  • Bumps may beat recency, but it isn't established. cq, whose kernel can peak at a distance, beats decay by about 0.01 on two seeds and ties on two (t = −1.7).
  • The far channel steadies the model. Its seed SD is 0.0017 (cq), close to exact's 0.0020, while w16's is 0.0070.
  • The short-context buckets are small. Targets 1–30 of each chunk (300 in all) read worse in the far arms (1.30 against 1.27 for w16); targets 31–142 (1,120) read better (cq 1.290, exact 1.289, w16 1.313). Neither bucket bears on the comparison.

Cost at this reach

The far channel's point is a state that doesn't grow with the reach. At a reach of 128 it saves nothing. Layer 2's keys and values for offsets 16–127 are 112 × 4 heads × 32 = 14,336 numbers. The channel's state is 8 × 4 × 272 = 8,704 real numbers for decay (8 atoms, each a 16 × 16 state plus a 16-entry normalizer, per head). For cq it is 64 × 4 × 272 ≈ 70k real numbers: the 64 products λ_j conj(λ_l) are 8 real atoms and 28 conjugate pairs of complex ones. So cq pays for itself only past a reach of about 550 characters, and decay past about 70. In training, the parallel form costs more than exact attention here: 34 ms (decay) and 42 ms (cq) per step, against 21 ms.

Caveats

  • Diagnostic evaluation, not lm-eval byte PPL. It is the same for every arm, and its absolute values aren't comparable with canonical PPL.
  • One size and one reach: d64/L2, a channel in layer 2 only, offsets 16–127. The efficiency case, a reach where keeping the keys and values costs more than the fixed state, is untested.
  • Not on the trie. This is a plain transformer. In AGPT, a trie node merges occurrences that share their last 16 characters but not their older text. A far channel makes each occurrence's prediction depend on that older text, so the node's occurrences no longer share a gradient. Putting the channel into AGPT would break the per-node aggregation unless the channel's input is shared per node, which is a separate design question.
  • The far arms have about 10% more parameters (118,885 and 120,965 against 108,481). The extra parameters see only offsets 16–127, so any gain they give comes from the older text. exact, with the fewest parameters, is still best.

Next, if wanted

  • A longer reach (512 or 1024), where the fixed state is smaller than the keys and values, to see whether the 0.010 shortfall holds.
  • More seeds, or a sharper test, for cq against decay.
